# UX Exploration

UX Exploration turns a user problem into evidence-grounded experience directions without prematurely implementing one.

## Mode

iterative

## Goal

- outcome: a mapped user journey, critical states, distinct design directions, tradeoffs, and a recommended decision or experiment
- acceptance: directions address the user need across relevant states and make usability, accessibility, feasibility, and uncertainty visible
- stop: the user problem is not established, essential evidence is unavailable, or value tradeoffs require a user or product decision

## Required Routes

Read every route below before Step 1. A route that cannot be read is a blocker to report, not a step to skip.

- [journey mapping, state coverage, design comparison, review, and handoff capability](../../skills/experience-design/SKILL.md) - #Skill #Design #UX

## Constraints

- Do not implement candidate directions in this workflow.
- Separate observed user needs, inferred needs, candidate ideas, and accepted decisions.
- Include accessibility and failure or recovery states from the start, not as a final add-on.
- Reuse established product language and interaction patterns unless divergence is justified.

## Steps

1. Establish the target users, job or problem, desired outcome, evidence, constraints, and success signal.
2. Inspect the current experience, product patterns, content, platform constraints, and relevant prior decisions.
3. Map the primary journey and the entry, empty, loading, success, error, permission, interruption, and recovery states that apply.
4. Identify the highest-friction moments, accessibility risks, unclear decisions, and evidence gaps.
5. Develop a small set of materially different experience directions at the fidelity needed for the decision.
6. Compare discoverability, comprehension, effort, feedback, control, accessibility, consistency, feasibility, and risk.
7. Recommend a direction when evidence supports one. Otherwise, define the smallest prototype, research question, or decision that resolves uncertainty.
8. Record accepted decisions and prepare a design-review or implementation-handoff input only when requested.

## Loop

Repeat steps 2 through 7 as evidence or feedback changes the journey, states, or tradeoffs. Stop when a direction is accepted, a resolving experiment is defined, or user input is required.

## Outputs

- user problem, evidence, constraints, and success signal
- journey and applicable state map
- distinct experience directions and tradeoffs
- recommendation, decision request, or experiment
- accepted decisions separated from candidates

## Completion

- [ ] user need and evidence are explicit
- [ ] relevant normal, edge, error, and recovery states are covered
- [ ] accessibility, consistency, and feasibility are considered
- [ ] no candidate direction was implemented by this workflow
- [ ] final response or handoff names this workflow
