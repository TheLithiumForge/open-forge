# Council

## Goal

Improve one consequential or genuinely uncertain decision through a small set of independent perspectives while preserving evidence, dissent, and final decision authority.

## Steps

1. Frame one decision, desired outcome, evaluation criteria, known constraints, authority, stopping condition, and available council budget. Stop when the recorded budget is zero.
2. Resolve cheap factual uncertainty before debate through bounded exploration or research.
3. Select two to four meaningfully different lenses. Prefer three when one grounded operator, one outsider or simplifier, and one risk lens cover the frontier.
4. Consume one council-budget unit and run one independent round in parallel when possible. Do not expose other positions or the preferred conclusion.
5. Require each position to separate evidence, assumptions, recommendation, strongest counterargument, tradeoffs, change conditions, and confidence in a compact return.
6. Resolve factual disagreements through authoritative evidence. Preserve value and tradeoff disagreements for the decision-maker.
7. Have the primary owner synthesize the strongest arguments, shared ground, material dissent, and recommendation. Do not vote or average positions.
8. Run one concise rebuttal round only when one or two unresolved disagreements could materially change the decision and an authorized council-budget unit remains. Otherwise stop.
9. Preserve reusable rationale only when consequence, surprise, recurrence, or future value justifies it.

## Completion

- The decision and criteria were explicit.
- Perspectives were independent and materially different.
- Facts, assumptions, and values were separated.
- One synthesis preserved dissent and did not use vote count.
- The decision-maker accepted a direction, delegated the exact decision, or received one visible unresolved frontier.
