# Council

## Goal

Improve one consequential or genuinely uncertain decision through independent perspectives while preserving factual provenance, dissent, and final user authority.

## Steps

1. Frame one decision, desired outcome, evaluation criteria, known constraints, and stopping condition.
2. Resolve cheap factual uncertainty before debate through bounded repository exploration or external research.
3. Select two to four meaningfully different lenses. A useful default is:
   - a cold outsider who does not assume the current system is correct;
   - a grounded operator who receives verified system constraints;
   - an adversary or radical simplifier who attacks the leading proposal.
4. Run the first round independently and in parallel when possible. Prefer the `advisor` agent for ordinary perspectives. Do not expose other members' responses or the preferred conclusion when independence matters.
5. Separate each response into facts, assumptions, values, recommendations, risks, evidence and reasoning, strongest alternatives, material tradeoffs, and conditions that would change the conclusion.
6. Resolve factual disagreements through authoritative evidence rather than debate. Preserve value disagreements for the decision-maker.
7. Have the primary owner synthesize the strongest arguments, shared ground, material dissent, and recommended direction. Do not decide by vote count.
8. If one or two material disagreements remain, run at most one concise rebuttal round focused only on those disagreements.
9. Use the stronger `challenger` only when the consequence justifies a high-cost adversarial seat.
10. Present the synthesis and decision frontier to the user. Preserve a sanitized rationale and meaningful post-round convergence or divergence in the active record and a matching Emerging Observation when reusable. After acceptance, integrate the result into the appropriate authoritative source when authorized.

## Completion

- The decision and criteria were explicit.
- First-round perspectives were independent.
- Facts and assumptions were distinguished.
- Material disagreement was preserved rather than averaged away.
- The primary owner produced one synthesis and recommendation.
- Each position and the synthesis preserve decisive reasoning, strongest alternatives, tradeoffs, and change conditions for later sanitized comparison.
- The user decided, delegated decision authority, or the exact unresolved frontier remains visible.
