# Guidance

## Role

Guidance preserves adaptable judgment for a recurring choice, tradeoff, or scenario.

It is useful when a preferred approach and its reasoning can improve future decisions, but relevant facts may legitimately justify another approach. Guidance reduces repeated reasoning without pretending that every occurrence has the same answer.

## Advisory Authority

Selected Guidance informs judgment. It does not bind behavior and does not silently outrank current facts, accepted direction, or a more authoritative source.

A deliberate adaptation is valid when its reason remains visible. Repeated adaptations may reveal that the Guidance needs refinement, a narrower scope, or a separate alternative. When behavior must be mandatory, the requirement belongs in a Directive or another applicable Axiom.

## Scope

Guidance states the positive situation in which its reasoning is useful. Recursive routes may narrow that situation by discipline, technology, project, decision area, or any other meaningful context.

More specific selected Guidance normally provides the better fit, but specialization does not erase broader context automatically. Conflicting advice is resolved through the current facts and authority model rather than by treating Guidance as an override hierarchy.

## Relationships

Guidance can explain how to choose among Patterns, when to use a Skill or Workflow, or what tradeoffs surround a Workspace destination. Those links preserve separate roles:

- A Directive states required behavior
- A Pattern provides an established inspectable shape
- A Skill performs specialized work
- A Workflow coordinates a defined goal
- Guidance helps judge what fits the situation

A decision preserves why an important choice was accepted. Guidance generalizes reusable judgment from one or more choices without replacing their historical rationale.

## Examples And Boundaries

Useful Guidance includes:

- criteria for choosing between two valid architectural approaches
- tradeoffs for introducing a dependency
- advice for adapting review depth to risk
- signals that a familiar Pattern does not fit

A mandatory naming rule is a Directive, not Guidance. A copy-ready document is a Template. A description of one accepted system state belongs in its current document rather than in Guidance.
