# Council 3 Reviews

The twelve Stage 2 peer reviews. Each reviewer ranked the eleven other proposals four times, once per deliverable, without knowing whose they were.

## Adoption Strategist (Proposal A)

/root - GPT-6 - high

### Critiques

- **Proposal B:** Carefully restores universal authority and maintenance guarantees, including their deadlines and reporting duties. Its 1,317-word loader also carries catalogue scaffolding and metadata reference material into every task, leaving substantial room for reducing startup reading through mandatory authoring triggers.

- **Proposal C:** Its explicit category triggers and condition checks give agents a particularly clear reading procedure. The broader loss audit remains incomplete, including Archived’s removal of current-authority metadata and Patterns’ optional and repeatable annotations.

- **Proposal D:** Provides the strongest audit beyond the 46 flagged rows and makes authoring references reachable without the CLI. Its ladder bundles continuity with Essentials and places specialized methods after Full Core, making independent capabilities look more coupled than their actual dependencies require.

- **Proposal E:** Takes upgrade compatibility seriously and makes bridge selection explicit. Restoring every selected catalogue’s old startup tag and the CLI Skill’s startup read reverses much of v2’s reading reduction, while the new bridge flag adds release scope.

- **Proposal F:** Clearly separates activation, management and sharing, with sensible independent Memory choices. Its v3 never explicitly says that patterns within one list are alternatives, leaving manual readers room to infer different matching behavior from the CLI.

- **Proposal G:** Identifies the saving-policy sharing problem and precisely distinguishes inspecting a nonmatch from applying it. Its first README link is conditioned on `src/**`, but the suggested test-explanation task supplies no matching working path, so the first demonstration may miss its intended document.

- **Proposal H:** Offers the clearest dependency model and puts relocated authoring obligations under a binding `## Instructions` heading. Its statement that unlisted files remain background needs reconciliation with its mandatory, unlisted README reference.

- **Proposal I:** Takes the two-file endpoint seriously and acknowledges the cost of promoting it into managed installation. However, it introduces another lifecycle boundary, and its restored Emerging capture rule lacks an explicit restoration of the default refresh trigger.

- **Proposal J:** Provides a strong introduction that moves quickly from a recognizable problem to a useful document link. Its implementation list does not cover the Memory-parent selection and empty-membership repairs needed to deliver its advertised Minimal behavior.

- **Proposal K:** Gives the most concrete implementation plan, including separate Memory-parent and empty-loader defects, continuation behavior and verification costs. Its 1,231-word loader still makes every task carry authoring mechanics that could be required immediately before authoring instead.

- **Proposal L:** Demonstrates that rules and accepted facts can remain in ordinary `docs/` files, including an explicit authority declaration. Its lossless audit still misses Archived’s obligation to remove metadata claiming former behavioral roles or current authority.

### Lossless verdicts in dispute

- **Loader L21–22, communication defaults:** **Restore.** Their compulsory default should survive until the maintainer explicitly changes it. Moving them into policies omitted by Minimal, as I proposes, changes reach rather than merely location.

- **Loader L28, L37, L57–58, authoring mechanics:** **Relocate to mandatory authoring Instructions.** D, H, J and L demonstrate a useful direction. Require reading and following the reference before the governed action, independently of Skills and the CLI.

- **Loader L46–49 and L95–97, vocabulary and labels:** **Relocate to the human guide.** Retaining explanations costs no compulsory startup reading and supports existing documentation and CLI terminology. Their removal is less consequential than losing a behavioral rule, but unnecessary.

- **Loader L85, nested-loading example:** **Relocate.** Its behavior is largely redundant, but the example remains useful for predicting what nested tags do.

- **Memory L27, defining-source updates:** **Relocate the shared deadline and accepted-source duty to the loader.** A, B and G avoid making those protections depend on Working or saving policy. Keep Memory-specific preservation instructions in Memory and avoid competing copies.

- **Memory L26, stated, observed and inferred:** **Restore without adding a new qualification.** E and G condition the distinction on its effect on trust. That may be reasonable policy, but the old second sentence did not contain that condition.

- **Emerging L17, candidate capture:** **Restore the obligation and its trigger.** Returning its words while leaving Emerging without its former refresh tag does not restore default reach.

- **Patterns L25, backlinks:** **Restore the original strength.** D and H change “should link back” into “must”. A lossless rewrite should preserve the distinction unless the maintainer accepts stronger requirements.

### Steal list

1. **L:** Demonstrate a rule and accepted fact in ordinary `docs/` files, showing that custom locations support behavior as well as discovery.
2. **H:** Put authoring rules under `## Instructions` and require them immediately before authoring, preserving force while reducing routine startup reading.
3. **K:** Treat omitted Memory-parent delivery and empty-loader preservation as separate implementation cases with separate proofs.
4. **G:** Keep enabled saving policy reachable and shareable while private Working record names remain private.
5. **C:** Avoid broad Memory exclusions that make adding one state unexpectedly require restoring its ancestor first.
6. **H:** Defer the frontmatter question when Minimal will write no routed metadata, removing a decision before the first useful result.
7. **D:** Audit Kept and Moved rows as well as Dropped and Partly, because optimistic classifications conceal real losses.
8. **B:** Compare equivalent obligations against plain `AGENTS.md`, so reduced duties cannot masquerade as improved usability or compliance.

### Best sentences

- **J:** “Open Forge gives your agent a reading list for the rules, docs and decisions your project already has.”
- **H:** “Memory states need Memory, but they do not require each other.”
- **C:** “Patterns within one list are alternatives.”
- **G:** “You may inspect a nonmatch for background without activating it.”
- **L:** “Your acceptance determines which project facts it may rely on.”

### Most dangerous idea

Making CLI Minimal an unmanaged two-file seed, as I proposes, introduces a promotion boundary that the normal installer does not need. Subsequent installation must distinguish an intentionally empty seed from an unmanaged brownfield tree and preserve its choices without lifecycle records.

Support the two-file setup through manual installation. Let CLI installation retain normal settings and ownership records. Saving two bookkeeping files does not justify a second installation lifecycle for the first stable release.

### Convergence

Most proposals independently agree that:

- One existing document should provide the first useful result.
- Documents should retain their locations, content and ownership.
- Minimal should select files through existing installer machinery.
- Presets should not become persisted runtime modes.
- Disabling, releasing management and deleting are distinct actions.
- General acceptance and maintenance duties must survive without Memory.
- Category safeguards belong where their consumers encounter them.
- Restoring Emerging capture requires restoring its refresh trigger.
- Empty Entries must remain empty during maintenance.
- Public startup descriptions need updating alongside the payload.
- Stranger usability remains unproven by historical test counts.
- Plain files remain complete, and agent compliance still needs review.

### Open conflicts

1. **Expanded loader versus required authoring reference.** B, C and K retain substantial authoring detail in startup. H and J move it behind an explicit authoring trigger. I recommend the latter, keeping universal authority and maintenance rules in the loader. Declare the reference part of the loader’s contract so it cannot be confused with a disabled branch.

2. **Original startup frequency versus required task triggers.** E restores selected catalogues and the CLI guide to startup. C and H preserve obligations through explicit triggers. I recommend required task triggers for category use and authoring, with unbroken automatic-read chains wherever automatic loading is required. Emerging is different because capture occurs at refresh boundaries, so its default refresh trigger should return.

3. **Managed Minimal versus an unmanaged seed.** H and K retain ordinary lifecycle records, while I introduces promotion from a seed. I recommend managed Minimal, existing bridge behavior and unchanged unattended defaults. Use explicit Minimal commands in the introduction, defer irrelevant metadata questions, and change the unqualified recommendation only after the reader trial.

4. **Markdown-only links versus explicit text leaves.** Recommend accepting additional local text leaves: `.mdc`, `.txt`, `.rst`, `.adoc`, `.asciidoc`, and extensionless `.windsurfrules` and `.clinerules` when they are files. `.md` already covers `CLAUDE.md`, Copilot instructions, conventions and typical ADRs.

   Keep recursive catalogues in Markdown. Link exact files rather than expanding globs or directories automatically. For other formats, entry metadata should control Open Forge loading without interpreting native activation fields or emulating another tool’s imports.

   This needs reader and source-model work plus Context, Index and Doctor parity tests. The [current linked-destination resolver](src/cli/framework/OpenForge.Cli.Framework/Framework/Sources/Loading/SourceGeneratedDestinationResolver.cs) explicitly rejects non-Markdown suffixes. Documentation alone cannot provide this capability.

### Revised position

I would adopt the name `minimal`, retain normal CLI lifecycle records, and defer its frontmatter question.

I would move authoring-only mechanics into binding README Instructions with an explicit read-and-follow trigger. Shared authority, maintenance and communication duties would remain in the loader.

I would add L’s intermediate example using an ordinary rule file and accepted specification under `docs/`, before introducing categories. This better demonstrates customizability.

I would retain the existing inherited marker verbatim, avoiding the inspection and scaffolding compatibility work caused by changing its punctuation.

Finally, I would include the saving-policy sharing repair, require a matching working path in the first scoped demonstration, and extend loader links to the bounded text formats above. Those changes make the adoption claim more concrete without adding a harness.

## Unix Minimalist (Proposal I)

/root - GPT-6 - high

### Critiques

**Proposal A.** Its strongest contribution is tying adoption to an observable result: a fresh agent uses an existing document, with additional repairs beyond the 46 flagged rows. Its 1,349-word loader keeps substantial catalogue-authoring detail in every startup, and its procedure makes pre-load condition checking less explicit than the stronger drafts.

**Proposal B.** It makes the essential distinction that “a rule relocated somewhere its consumer never visits is still lost,” and supports the empty-list problem with source evidence. Its continuity rung bundles Working, Emerging and Archived, making a simple need to resume work appear to require several additional responsibilities.

**Proposal C.** It preserves important details such as mandatory Pattern shapes and explicitly states depth-first reading order. Its wider loss audit is incomplete, with no restoration of explicit permission before creating a new top-level Memory state, despite that obligation disappearing behind a Moved classification.

**Proposal D.** This is the strongest compatibility audit, covering hidden losses, required authoring reads, marker compatibility and documentation drift. Its ladder then makes continuity synonymous with Essentials and places specialized methods after Full Core, which weakens the otherwise convincing case for independent adoption.

**Proposal E.** Making host-bridge selection explicit gives brownfield users useful control over which instruction files installation touches. Reinstating unconditional catalogue startup tags and the CLI Skill’s startup read sacrifices a major v2 improvement instead of preserving those obligations through narrower, explicit triggers.

**Proposal F.** It explains nested removal, management release and independent Memory states particularly well. Its no-CLI quick start copies the full loader without explicitly clearing entries for uninstalled categories, so following it literally can leave missing required catalogues.

**Proposal G.** Its strongest contribution is identifying the interaction between private Working contents and the packaged saving policy, with changes spanning sharing facts and ownership compatibility. Its first example conditions CONTRIBUTING on `src/**`, while the trial request supplies no matching working path, so the promised first required read can remain pending.

**Proposal H.** Its dependency graph and removal rules provide the clearest account of components remaining usable independently. Its saving-policy repair needs a more explicit contract and cost because a Git-ignore exception alone cannot overcome `SourceSharing.Includes`, which also filters that file from generated navigation.

**Proposal J.** Its introduction identifies concrete points where an impatient reader stops and supplies an approachable, copyable first rule. However, its installer specification does not defer the frontmatter question that the current first-install flow asks, leaving the terminal journey inconsistent with its strongest onboarding criticism.

**Proposal K.** Its implementation plan is unusually concrete about physical effects, JSON, verification and estimated release cost. Excluding the entire Memory parent creates an additional restoration boundary, forcing a Custom configuration workaround where an ordinary one-state Route Init would otherwise be the natural next step.

**Proposal L.** Its scoped rule and explicitly accepted fact in `docs/` are the best demonstration that useful adoption does not require Open Forge categories. Its continuity example nevertheless adds Directives and Crystallized alongside Working, obscuring the smaller Working-only setup that the framework should support.

### Lossless verdicts in dispute

Disagreement concentrates on placement and reach. Most category-specific restorations receive broadly similar treatment.

- **Loader L21–22, communication defaults — Restore.** Keep their binding status until the maintainer explicitly approves making them optional, with upgrades preserving existing choices.
- **Loader L28, links and tags — Relocate with a required authoring trigger.** The trigger must cover linked project documents as well as files inside `.agents/`.
- **Loader L37, inherited marker — Relocate with a required catalogue-authoring read.** Preserve the existing literal spelling unless changing its punctuation has a demonstrated benefit.
- **Loader L57–58, nested scopes and selective structure — Relocate.** Keep application and inheritance behavior in the loader, while authors encounter structural permissions before changing catalogues.
- **Loader L46–49 and L95–97, vocabulary and labels — Relocate.** A human reference costs no startup context and remains useful while the CLI and documentation use those terms.
- **Loader L85, nested-loading example — Relocate.** Its execution behavior follows the active-parent rule, but the example still helps people predict loading.
- **Memory L27, update deadlines — Restore in Memory.** Keep general accepted-source and Evergreen maintenance independently available in the loader, so removing saving does not remove those duties.
- **Emerging L17, capture at refresh — Restore with its trigger.** Restoring the sentence without restoring the selected component’s refresh reach preserves wording rather than behavior.

### Steal list

1. **Proposal L — Show a rule and accepted fact remaining in `docs/`.** This proves customizability through a working example before introducing categories.
2. **Proposal D — Require authoring guidance immediately before authoring.** This preserves binding requirements while reducing unrelated startup reading.
3. **Proposal H — Publish the small dependency graph.** Users need to know that Working and Crystallized require their parent, not their siblings.
4. **Proposal B — Pin empty-list preservation before shipping Minimal.** The current projection planner returns structural roots for an empty listed set.
5. **Proposal G — Preserve packaged policies alongside private records.** Both generated navigation and Git sharing must keep the enabled saving policy reachable without exposing private record names.
6. **Proposal J — Perform no adoption inside omitted routes.** A minimal install should not unexpectedly normalize or incorporate an existing native Skill tree.
7. **Proposal H — Defer the metadata-form question until metadata is needed.** The first trial should reach its useful document link before asking about representation.
8. **Proposal I — Retain a two-file manual endpoint.** Experienced users should be able to adopt routing without also adopting CLI lifecycle management.

### Best sentences

- **Proposal D:** “Your documents stay where they are.”
- **Proposal H:** “Listing a file makes it available to Open Forge.”
- **Proposal C:** “Read its required children before continuing to the next entry.”
- **Proposal J:** “A reading link does not approve everything in a document or grant permission to edit it.”

### Most dangerous idea

**Proposal E’s wholesale restoration of catalogue startup tags and the CLI Skill’s startup read.** This treats the old reading schedule as inseparable from the obligations it delivered, bringing task-independent reading back into selected installations.

Preserve existing selections during upgrades. For fresh setups, retain v2’s task-based discovery and make the relevant use triggers explicit. Otherwise Open Forge becomes smaller on disk while recovering much of its old context cost.

### Convergence

- Most proposals recommend one existing document as the first useful adoption.
- Categories and named record types remain optional.
- Existing documentation stays in place without added frontmatter.
- Reading and installation do not establish acceptance.
- Disabling behavior, releasing management and deleting files are distinct operations.
- Most proposals use concrete route selections rather than a persisted runtime mode.
- Most recognize that the ledger’s Kept and Moved classifications also require review.
- Fresh-agent observations and maintenance checks are needed alongside word counts.

### Open conflicts

1. **Two-file CLI seed versus managed Minimal.** I recommend shipping managed Minimal through the existing installer first, while retaining the two-file manual route. A stateless CLI seed introduces an ownership and promotion boundary that deserves separate proof. The plan must disclose every administrative file and preserve existing host choices.

2. **Wholesale startup restoration versus behavior-preserving task triggers.** I recommend task triggers for authoring, advice and capabilities, with original refresh policies preserved for existing selected components during upgrades. Communication defaults should remain binding until their optionalization receives an explicit decision. Compatibility should preserve obligations without forcing every fresh installation to reproduce the old read schedule.

3. **Category-first growth versus documents-first growth.** I recommend L’s documents-first example followed by H’s capability and dependency explanation. A new rule or accepted fact can remain in an existing source. Working alone must be a complete option, and Extensions should not appear to require Full Core unless their actual dependencies do.

4. **Markdown-only links versus explicit text-file links.** I recommend accepting explicitly linked UTF-8 text leaves, including `.mdc`, `.txt`, `.rst`, `.adoc`, and extensionless regular files such as `.windsurfrules` and `.clinerules`. `CLAUDE.md`, `.github/copilot-instructions.md` and `CONVENTIONS.md` already fit Markdown support.

   Keep non-Markdown sources opaque. Entry metadata controls Open Forge reading, while the document’s stated scope still governs application. Do not rewrite native frontmatter or emulate another tool’s activation engine. Keep catalogue discovery Markdown-based, and use explicit files or an existing index for `docs/adr/`, without recursive directory ingestion. This needs linked-reader and resolver changes plus parity tests, not a separate parser for each native format.

### Revised position

I would ship a managed Minimal preset before adding stateless CLI installation and automatic promotion. The two-file manual option remains useful.

For managed setups, I would replace the always-read authoring policy with D and H’s explicit read-before-authoring requirement. I would keep communication defaults binding pending a maintainer decision and explicitly preserve Emerging’s refresh reach when that component remains selected.

I would adopt L’s category-free rule-and-fact example, require G’s sharing and fresh-checkout proof, and add bounded text-leaf support for existing native instruction files. These changes make the proposal more useful to brownfield projects and more realistic for the first stable release.
