# Opt-In Council Reviews

Stage 2 output, unchanged except for removed local paths and runtime labels. Reviewers saw proposals only as letters and did not rank their own. The [record](record.md) holds the aggregate ranking.

## Audience Strategist (Proposal G)

#### Critiques

**Proposal A.** Its strongest contribution is separating communication, capture, and verification policies from storage categories, while explicitly preserving accepted project requirements when a module is disabled. Its treatment of inactive catalogues also closes an ambiguity left by several proposals. The main weakness is its default: an empty Directives scaffold and a relatively substantial loader still precede the first useful connection to project knowledge, while three new behavior packs expand implementation scope.

**Proposal B.** It provides unusually concrete replacement texts and identifies deliberate policy removals rather than presenting every deletion as harmless compression. Its experimental requirement to “Match enabled policies across versions” is particularly valuable. Durable customization is less completely resolved, since companions and exclusions still carry much of the burden and preservation of authored activation membership is not clearly specified.

**Proposal C.** The explicit reading list removes substantial runtime bookkeeping, and its versioned interpretation and result-header check take compatibility seriously. However, replacing inherited conditions and catalogue authority creates a second loading contract with difficult migration and ongoing maintenance costs. Direct loader entries under existing semantics can deliver much of its initial benefit, so the additional semantic change needs comparative evidence before adoption.

**Proposal D.** Its four independent adoption paths make optionality concrete, and “Connect one source” supplies a useful first action without requiring a new taxonomy. It also obtains substantial simplification while retaining Council 1’s loading semantics. Its most important gap is the activation boundary: “read its entrypoint ancestry” needs an explicit qualification explaining how lookup of retained inactive catalogues avoids reactivating their former rules.

**Proposal E.** It combines a small install, an authoring guide, and direct documentation links without introducing another loading mode. It also catches Council 1’s accidental strengthening of the Template selection trigger. Its main weakness is coupling the routing change to a changed Essentials preset and broad preservation of edited and missing managed files, which increases compatibility and lifecycle scope before the adoption model has been tested.

**Proposal F.** It makes the strongest case for preserving acceptance, source authority, and continuity rather than reducing Open Forge to navigation alone. Its alternatives table explains what users retain and lose when choosing another implementation. However, automatic preservation remains in the universal loader, so removing Memory does not remove the capture or handoff obligation, which falls short of independently switchable opinions.

**Proposal H.** Its strongest contribution is treating the opinion as the unit of behavioral choice and requiring modules to declare dependencies and behavior when absent. This directly addresses removal that otherwise leaves dangling procedures or recreates omitted storage. Its weakness is the size of the proposed module and policy system, including installation metadata and additional routing surfaces, which risks rebuilding the complexity being removed unless introduced through a few demonstrated cases.

#### Steal list

- **Proposal D: Connect one source.** The final design needs a first success that uses existing project knowledge without requiring categories, relocation, or metadata changes.
- **Proposal A: Separate activation from management and deletion.** Users must be able to stop a procedure while retaining their knowledge and understanding what maintenance will subsequently do.
- **Proposal H: Declare requirements and absence behavior.** Every independently selectable component must explain what depends on it and what happens when it is omitted.
- **Proposal C: Return complete bodies with selection explanations.** CLI acceleration becomes tangible when one call supplies usable context, reasons, and unresolved conditions rather than another navigation task.
- **Proposal B: Compare matched policies.** Reliability experiments must distinguish better execution from simply removing obligations.

#### Most dangerous idea

Keeping automatic capture in the irreducible loader, as Proposal F does, would preserve a central source of takeover. Its fallback to a handoff in the reply preserves the obligation even when storage is absent.

Acceptance discipline can remain part of the reading contract without requiring every unfinished task to produce a continuity artifact. Capture should remain valuable through an explicit policy that users can enable independently of its destination.

#### Audience verdict

The strongest initial audience is maintainers and small teams using agents in existing repositories, especially when rules, documentation, and decisions are repeatedly rediscovered across sessions. Solo developers and monorepo contributors are credible secondary audiences, while fleet orchestration is an optional specialization. The “hobby without an audience” critique fits the current breadth and onboarding, but the repository demonstrates recurring jobs rather than validated market demand.

#### Convergence

- Plain files define correct behavior independently of the CLI.
- Existing documents should participate without relocation or compulsory frontmatter.
- Required reads must be visibly reachable without discretionary discovery of an optional parent.
- Most proposals retain Council 1’s inherited loading semantics and condition matching.
- Unknown working paths leave relevant conditions pending until affected work.
- The CLI usage manual should leave startup context.
- Concrete command triggers and useful batched output should encourage CLI use.
- Categories, filing conventions, and development methods should become optional.
- Recording, tagging, linking, and moving material do not establish acceptance.
- Activation, maintenance ownership, sharing, and deletion need distinct treatment.
- Existing installations should retain behavior until a reviewed migration.
- Experiments should inspect actual reads, resulting behavior, and successful opt-outs.

#### Open conflicts

**Loading contract: checked chains with direct entries versus `flat-v1`.** Recommend retaining checked chains while making direct entries the simplest authoring path. Benchmark C’s flat model as an alternative, especially on larger configurations, before funding a second semantic contract.

**Recommended default: connect an existing source versus an empty kernel versus baseline continuity.** Recommend D’s connection-led default, with an explicitly empty configuration supported. Offer continuity immediately as a separate choice, usable through an existing note without adopting four Memory states.

**Customization durability: preserve edited and missing managed files versus retain managed replacement with companions or user-owned replacements.** Protect authored activation membership immediately. Gate the broader Update change separately, while supporting links to user-owned replacements and clearly explaining managed-file behavior. Safe deactivation should not depend on redesigning every Framework and Extension update operation.

#### Revised position

I would change my proposal in six ways:

- Replace the fixed Maps default with D’s direct connection to one existing source. Offer an index when grouping becomes useful.
- Specify that inspecting an inactive catalogue does not activate its Axioms, while accepted project requirements and independently loaded host instructions remain applicable.
- Adopt H’s dependency and absence contracts at component boundaries, initially proving them with routing and optional continuity rather than introducing the full module catalogue.
- Make standalone resumption available through an existing task note, independently of Memory categories and Planning.
- Separate the initial linked-source and activation work from the broader change to edited-file handling and any split of the Planning package.
- Strengthen the experiment with B’s matched-policy comparison, keeping the plain `AGENTS.md` baseline and testing a changed working path, a pause, and an opt-out that survives maintenance.

## Minimalist Adopter (Proposal D)

#### Critiques

**Proposal A**

Its strongest contribution is the distinction between retaining files and activating their behavior, supported by separate communication, capture and verification choices. It also preserves Council 1’s loading mechanics without introducing another mode. The loader still requires frontmatter for “routed Markdown” without clearly exempting existing linked documents, leaving an important adoption promise ambiguous.

**Proposal B**

The complete replacement payload and sentence audit make this unusually concrete and reviewable. Its experiment also correctly requires matching enabled policies, so removing obligations cannot masquerade as improved compliance. However, activation preservation is less explicit than in other proposals, particularly the interaction between “regenerate membership”, manually removed loader entries and subsequent Index operations. The opt-out procedure remains distributed across link edits, source edits, companions and exclusions.

**Proposal C**

This offers the clearest reduction in runtime bookkeeping: required reading becomes independent row evaluation, with no ancestor recovery. Its explicit `flat-v1` result header also addresses incompatible CLI output more carefully than most proposals. The serious cost is changing inherited authority and conditions alongside loading semantics, which requires substantial migration and Extension work. It is a strong benchmark candidate, but should pass Council 1’s equivalence gate before becoming the default.

**Proposal E**

This provides the best balance of smaller adoption, existing loading semantics, metadata-free documentation links and manual authoring support outside startup. Its discovery that Council 1 strengthened the Templates trigger is particularly valuable evidence that shortening can accidentally increase control. The broad promise that Update preserves edited and missing files expands the work into managed lifecycle policy, which should be separated from the first routing release or introduced through an explicit compatibility boundary.

**Proposal F**

Its strongest contribution is defending acceptance, authoritative sources and honest reliability as valuable substance worth preserving. However, the loader’s unconditional instruction to preserve unfinished work remains active even after Memory is removed. The seven-file recommendation and mandatory capture therefore leave substantially more coupling than the maintainer’s direction warrants.

**Proposal G**

This has the sharpest positioning and the strongest first-success criterion: an existing project document used correctly in a fresh session. Comparing that experience against plain `AGENTS.md` is an especially useful test of whether Open Forge earns its additional files. Its delivery scope is less focused, combining routing, update behavior and possible package restructuring. Those changes should be independently shippable so the audience hypothesis can be tested cheaply.

**Proposal H**

Its strongest contribution is the explicit contract for what each module provides, requires and leaves behind when absent. Separating storage containers from capture, acceptance, conformance and maintenance policies directly addresses hidden coupling. Its serious risk is introducing a large category-by-policy configuration system, including new dependency and installation machinery. Those contracts should support a simple capability chooser rather than become the adopter’s new curriculum.

#### Steal list

1. **A and D: User-owned activation membership.** The final design must preserve the loader’s chosen roots through maintenance, allowing behavior to be disabled without deleting useful knowledge.
2. **H: Explicit absence contracts.** Each optional component must explain what still works without it, making independence a testable property.
3. **C: Declare the reading contract in Context output.** Models need a visible way to reject incompatible computed context and fall back to the files.
4. **E: Preserve conditional triggers during simplification.** Moving Templates on demand must not turn “when copy-ready starting content would help” into a check before every artifact.
5. **G: Make first success reuse an existing document.** This demonstrates immediate value to an established project before asking anyone to learn the taxonomy.

#### Most dangerous idea

**Putting automatic continuity capture in the kernel, as F does.**

“Before pausing unfinished work, preserve the goal, current state, next step and blockers” remains mandatory even when the user removes Memory. The fallback handoff in the reply preserves that obligation rather than disabling it. This recreates the central problem: someone adopting scoped rules inherits another working method, and rejecting that method requires rewriting the supposedly irreducible contract.

Acceptance boundaries belong in the kernel. Automatic capture belongs in an explicitly selected policy.

#### Audience verdict

The strongest common target is developers maintaining established repositories whose agents repeatedly miss existing rules, documentation or past decisions. Solo developers resuming work and teams navigating multiple packages are closely related audiences. The hobby critique is fair about prioritization and adoption overhead, while the repository establishes credible user needs and leaves market demand unvalidated.

#### Convergence

Most proposals independently support these directions:

- Teach the reading procedure before routing vocabulary.
- Keep the CLI optional and provide complete plain-file behavior.
- Let existing documentation participate at its current location.
- Remove compulsory CLI-guide reading from startup.
- Make required reading reachable without discretionary parent selection.
- Keep unknown file conditions pending until affected work can be identified.
- Separate recording, loading and tagging from acceptance.
- Make Memory organization and communication preferences optional.
- Distinguish disabling behavior from releasing management or deleting files.
- Preserve chosen activation membership through maintenance.
- Retain current overwrite behavior while postponing another replacement language.
- Preserve installed workspace behavior until a reviewed migration.
- Evaluate actual reads and outcomes rather than treating shorter text as proof.

There is broad agreement on these principles, but substantially less agreement on default composition and the loading mechanism.

#### Open conflicts

1. **Checked chains versus `flat-v1`.**  
   A, B, E, F, G and H retain inherited loading semantics. C replaces them with independent required rows and navigation-only catalogues. I recommend checked chains for the next release, with direct links as the simplest authoring pattern. Benchmark C’s alternative using Council 1’s gate before changing authority and condition composition.

2. **What the recommended installation should do immediately.**  
   The choices include an empty kernel, a Directives scaffold, continuity capture, or an index of existing project sources. I recommend a small kernel connected to one useful existing source, with optional authoring help. Offer continuity explicitly, and preserve the ability to choose an empty endpoint without presenting emptiness as the main onboarding experience.

3. **Activation preservation versus a broader Update policy change.**  
   Some proposals preserve current managed replacement behavior, while C, E and G preserve edited files and offer upstream differences. Protect user-owned loader membership and imported documents immediately. Treat changed behavior for existing managed Framework and Extension files as a separately reviewed migration, while offering new user-owned starters without automatic replacement. Missing required targets must still be reported.

#### Revised position

I would make four changes to Proposal D:

- Adopt A and H’s explicit activation boundary. Reading an inactive catalogue for evidence must not activate its former policies, while deactivation must not revoke accepted project requirements or host-native instructions.
- Add H’s small absence contracts to optional components and their acceptance checks. Keep these out of startup context and avoid exposing a large policy matrix during installation.
- Offer E’s authoring guide outside startup, covering manual entry maintenance, direct documentation links and durable customization. The two-file endpoint remains available.
- Adopt C’s visible compatibility declaration in Context output and B’s matched-policy comparisons in the experiment. Test equivalent obligations before measuring reductions, and include E’s Template-trigger regression as a concrete preservation case.

I would retain direct ordinary-file links and existing chain semantics as the initial implementation, while keeping the flat-list alternative open to evidence.

## Essence Guardian (Proposal F)

#### Critiques

**Proposal A.** Its strongest contribution is separating activation from file retention, while explicitly stating that deactivation does not revoke accepted project requirements. The loader also gives a practical CLI shortcut and a clear fallback. Its weakness is introducing communication, capture and verification packs before establishing that users need those packaging boundaries, which risks replacing the existing taxonomy with another configuration system.

**Proposal B.** The detailed text audit and experiment that matches enabled obligations across versions are valuable contributions. Its weakest point is the recommended empty installation: “A guided first link must prove that tradeoff,” but that guidance is not part of the two shipped files or a concrete installation step. The shortest loader therefore comes with the least convincing first useful experience.

**Proposal C.** This most directly reduces inherited-condition bookkeeping, and its explicit reading-mode handshake takes CLI compatibility seriously. The serious cost is changing both rule inheritance and loading semantics, requiring inherited prose obligations as well as conditions to be reconstructed during migration. It deserves a comparative experiment, but making it the release destination would place the largest compatibility project on the critical path.

**Proposal D.** “Make the unit of adoption one useful link” gives the clearest route from installation to value, and its independent adoption recipes address the maintainer’s request concretely. It also preserves existing inheritance while allowing ordinary documentation to participate directly. Its main weakness is tolerating framework-required reads hidden in prose, since checking incoming activation links cannot establish independence if another active instruction silently requires the disabled capability.

**Proposal E.** This combines durable customization, a small manual authoring guide and careful preservation of existing loading semantics. Its Templates example is particularly useful evidence: Council 1 strengthened a conditional recommendation into an obligation before every artifact creation. Its largest weakness is coupling the adoption redesign to a broad Update contract change, increasing migration and stale-policy risks when protecting authored activation membership could be delivered separately.

**Proposal G.** This gives the strongest primary audience and positioning, including the useful challenge that ordinary `AGENTS.md` may already be sufficient. Its proposed first success, correctly using an existing document in a fresh session, is a better product demonstration than a framework inventory. The weakness is requiring an initially empty Maps layer that largely duplicates the loader’s navigation role, so the third file needs to demonstrate an advantage over D’s direct link.

**Proposal H.** The explicit “provides, requires, when absent” contract is the strongest treatment of independent removal and missing destinations. It correctly separates storage, capture, acceptance and maintenance behavior. Its serious flaw is turning that analysis into roughly twenty selectable modules, which adds authoring, installation and dependency machinery before demonstrating that users benefit from so many boundaries. Putting the CLI trigger behind another optional adapter also makes acceleration depend on an additional discovery decision.

#### Steal list

1. **Proposal D: connect one useful existing source.** The default should demonstrate value through the user’s own knowledge before asking them to adopt organization or methods.
2. **Proposal A: distinguish deactivation, retention and accepted requirements.** Users must be able to disable framework procedures without deleting knowledge or accidentally treating established requirements as revoked.
3. **Proposal H: state what each optional capability requires and what happens when absent.** These small contracts prevent omitted Memory states or other components from being recreated through hidden dependencies.
4. **Proposal E: audit obligation strength during simplification.** The verified Templates regression shows why moving something on demand must not quietly make its instructions more demanding.
5. **Proposal F: report when continuation state was not saved.** When a handoff is needed but no writable destination is configured, an explicit unsaved handoff preserves honesty without manufacturing folders.

#### Most dangerous idea

Proposal C’s removal of inherited rule authority is the highest-risk idea. “Folder ancestry adds no rules” simplifies reading, but migrating to it can preserve every expected file read while losing the scope or force of an inherited Axiom.

C responsibly proposes versioning, extraction of inherited rules and reviewed migration. Those safeguards make it a credible experiment, but they do not establish semantic equivalence. Keep it outside the release’s critical path until trials compare resulting behavior and rule application, rather than only required-read sets.

#### Audience verdict

Open Forge’s clearest initial audience is maintainers of existing repositories who use coding agents and already have instructions, documentation or decisions worth reusing. Mixed-language teams and developers returning across sessions are concrete adjacent audiences, while coordinated agent operations belong in optional methods. The hobby critique holds as a criticism of positioning and unvalidated breadth, but the combined repository evidence supports a coherent problem rather than an absence of purpose.

#### Convergence

- All eight proposals support complete plain-file operation without a required CLI or new harness.
- All support ordinary documentation participating at its existing location.
- All distinguish reading from acceptance, ownership and permission.
- All favor a reading procedure before framework vocabulary.
- All reject compulsory startup reading of the full CLI guide.
- All preserve a distinction between missing configured sources and intentionally omitted capabilities.
- Most retain existing loading tags, inherited rules and same-file condition matching.
- Most want authored activation choices protected from Index and Update.
- Most make automatic capture and the four-state Memory organization independently avoidable.
- All propose observing actual reads and behavior, with users outside this repository, rather than treating shorter text as proof.

#### Open conflicts

1. **Default experience: connected source, continuity starter or empty router.** D and G emphasize existing knowledge, C and F retain continuity defaults, while B recommends an empty kernel. Recommend connecting one existing source during adoption, with a clearly offered continuity option and a larger preset for users who want it. The empty kernel should remain supported, but should not be the only first-run outcome.

2. **Required-reading structure: checked chains or a flat manifest.** Most proposals retain tagged chains, while C replaces inherited scheduling and authority with direct registration. Recommend direct loader entries for independent required sources, checked chains for useful grouping, and a benchmarked flat alternative. This captures much of C’s reduced decision burden without first requiring semantic migration.

3. **Customization lifecycle: managed replacement with companions or preservation of direct edits.** C, E and G propose preserving edited files, while several others retain the existing maintenance model. Protect authored loader membership immediately, preserve current companion semantics, and evaluate edited-file preservation as a separate lifecycle change with source diffs and explicit restoration. Successful opt-out should not depend on completing that broader Update redesign.

#### Revised position

I would change the recommended entry experience to D’s connected-source setup and retain the seven-file organization as an explicit continuity preset. The trust distinctions should remain consistent across both, while automatic capture becomes a selected behavior rather than an unavoidable consequence of using the kernel.

I would make loader membership explicitly user-owned and adopt A’s distinction between disabling procedures and revoking accepted requirements. Removing a loading tag must also be described accurately: it changes reading timing, not necessarily the force of instructions when subsequently adopted.

I would adopt H’s dependency contracts without immediately creating its complete module catalogue. New optional capabilities should declare their destinations, required reads and absent behavior, while legacy prose dependencies receive a visible migration path.

Finally, I would make direct documentation participation work without requiring a new `docs/_docs.md`, retain E’s manual authoring guidance outside startup, and add C’s explicit CLI compatibility signal. The release should first prove one useful source, one scoped rule and one successful opt-out, then expand from that evidence.

## No-CLI Realist (Proposal C)

#### Critiques

**Proposal A.** Its strongest contribution is distinguishing activation from management, retained knowledge and accepted project requirements. Separating communication, capture and verification also makes behavioral preferences independently selectable. Its most serious gap is allowing additional required reads through prose, which leaves both CLI and plain-file readers manually completing a dependency set that the structure cannot fully explain.

**Proposal B.** The complete replacement payload and explicit accounting of policy deletions make this unusually reviewable and buildable. Its requirement to compare versions with matching enabled policies is particularly valuable. Its most serious weakness is retaining replace-on-update behavior, so an ordinary policy edit remains temporary unless users understand companions or management exclusions.

**Proposal D.** “Make the unit of adoption one useful link” gives the clearest first success and demonstrates four genuinely separate adoption paths. It preserves existing loading semantics while removing unnecessary prerequisites around ordinary documents. Its most serious weakness appears in grown workspaces, where inherited conditions, ancestor recovery and prose-required reads still demand substantial model bookkeeping beyond the simple adoption example.

**Proposal E.** Preserving authored loader membership and edited files makes user ownership operational, rather than dependent on learning exceptions. Its Templates finding is also correct: Council 1 changed a conditional suggestion into an obligation before every new artifact. Its most serious weakness is that the default delivers an empty list and an authoring guide, leaving the first useful connection and the advantage over ordinary `AGENTS.md` unproven.

**Proposal F.** It makes the strongest case for preserving acceptance boundaries, source authority and continuity as valuable product commitments. Its most serious flaw is turning continuity into mandatory capture behavior in the kernel. Returning an unsaved handoff when no destination exists preserves that obligation even after users decline the proposed storage conventions.

**Proposal G.** It identifies the clearest primary audience and offers the strongest positioning test: compare against plain `AGENTS.md`, then narrow the audience if the extra framework does not help. Its most serious gap is inactive-source handling: the loader binds loaded entrypoint Axioms without explicitly stating H’s protection against an inactive catalogue regaining policy effects through search. That boundary needs resolution before “disabled stays disabled” is dependable.

**Proposal H.** It gives the most complete account of independent opinions, dependency disclosure and retained files that no longer activate policies. Requiring mandatory reads to appear in Entries also closes the prose-versus-CLI gap. Its most serious risk is moving complexity into installation through a large module catalogue, dependency contracts and matching installation metadata, creating another consistency problem before those abstractions have demonstrated their value.
